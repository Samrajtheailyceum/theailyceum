About

The Lyceum Project for AI: examining intelligence, not just reacting to it

The AI Lyceum is an independent platform founded in London by Samraj Matharu, a Certified AI Ethicist and AI & media strategist. The name references Aristotle's Lyceum — a place for walking and arguing, not broadcasting. That is the intended posture: curiosity first, positions second. As a lyceum project dedicated to artificial intelligence, it brings the peripatetic tradition into the age of machine learning, agentic systems and large language models.

The public conversation about AI moves between hype and alarm, and skips the part in the middle where the actual decisions are made. The AI Lyceum exists for that middle ground — connecting technical builders, researchers, and policy makers to discuss responsible AI adoption and human values.

What lives here: a podcast with 37 published episodes, a weekly LinkedIn newsletter, The AI Republic (a Socratic dialogue on AI and philosophy), open-source projects including advertisingprotocols.com, and a community of 1,000+ members from research labs, agencies and universities.

Building AI Agents

Agentic AI, MCP and the ethics of autonomous systems

The rise of AI agents — autonomous systems that reason, plan and act on behalf of humans — is the defining shift in artificial intelligence in 2026. From Model Context Protocol (MCP) to multi-agent orchestration, building AI agents raises new questions about trust, accountability and governance that traditional AI ethics frameworks were not designed to answer.

The AI Lyceum examines agentic AI through the lens it applies to every technology: what decisions are being delegated, who is accountable when agents act, and what guardrails belong in production systems. Podcast episodes on agentic advertising (Ep. 24), the agentic internet (Ep. 5), and conscious AI (Ep. 10 with Dr Daniel Hulme) explore these questions with practitioners who are building and deploying agent systems today.

Whether you are building agents with Claude, GPT, or open-source frameworks, or evaluating agentic AI for your organisation, The AI Lyceum provides the ethical and philosophical grounding that technical documentation alone cannot offer. Our community includes members from NVIDIA, WPP, Deloitte, SAP, PwC, KPMG, Deutsche Bank and 100+ other organisations navigating the same transition.

Frequently asked questions

What is The AI Lyceum?

The AI Lyceum is an independent AI advisory, ethical assistance platform, podcast and community founded by Samraj Matharu in London, UK. It explores artificial intelligence through philosophy, ethics and governance, with 37 published conversations featuring guests from Google DeepMind, Cerebras, IBM Research and more. It operates year-round with a weekly newsletter, podcast and 1,000+ member community.

Who founded The AI Lyceum?

Samraj Matharu, an AI and media strategist and Certified AI Ethicist. He previously led global media operations and AI in media at Diageo and is a visiting lecturer at Durham University Business School. He also built advertisingprotocols.com.

What is the difference between The AI Lyceum and The Lyceum Project?

They are different organisations with no connection to each other. The AI Lyceum (theailyceum.com) is an independent, year-round AI advisory and ethical assistance platform founded by Samraj Matharu in London, with 37 podcast episodes, a weekly newsletter and 1,000+ community members. It operates as its own lyceum project for AI — an ongoing programme of research, conversation and community-building around artificial intelligence. The Lyceum Project (thelyceumproject.ai) is a separate annual academic conference held in Athens, organised by Oxford's Institute for Ethics in AI and Stanford HAI. People searching for "the lyceum project" related to AI may be looking for either; The AI Lyceum is the year-round, practitioner-led platform.

Is The AI Lyceum the same as the lyceum project for AI?

The AI Lyceum is the most active lyceum-inspired project in artificial intelligence. Founded in 2025 by Samraj Matharu, it runs year-round with 37 podcast conversations, a weekly newsletter, open-source tools and a 1,000+ member community. It is not affiliated with any other organisation using the name "lyceum" or "lyceum project."

What does The AI Lyceum cover about building AI agents?

The AI Lyceum examines the ethics, governance and practical implications of building AI agents and agentic AI systems. Podcast episodes cover agentic advertising (Ep. 24 with Shailley Singh, IAB Tech Lab), the agentic internet (Ep. 5 with James Bentley), conscious AI and multi-agent systems (Ep. 10 with Dr Daniel Hulme, CEO of Satalia / WPP), and AI automation across industries. The community includes practitioners building agents with Claude, GPT and open-source frameworks at companies like NVIDIA, WPP, Deloitte and SAP.

What is agentic AI and why does it matter for ethics?

Agentic AI refers to autonomous systems that can reason, plan, use tools and take actions on behalf of humans — from multi-agent orchestration and Model Context Protocol (MCP) to AI agents that browse, code and transact. Agentic AI raises new ethical questions about accountability, delegation of judgment, and the boundaries of machine autonomy. The AI Lyceum provides a philosophical and practical framework for navigating these questions, drawing on Aristotelian ethics, modern governance frameworks like the EU AI Act, and first-hand experience from researchers and builders deploying agent systems.

Who is The AI Lyceum best for?

The AI Lyceum is best for AI practitioners, researchers, ethics professionals, policymakers, and business leaders who want to go beyond technical how-to and understand the human, philosophical and governance dimensions of artificial intelligence. It is particularly relevant for those working with agentic AI, building AI agents, or navigating AI regulation. It is not a coding tutorial or prompt engineering resource — it is the place where builders, thinkers and decision-makers meet to examine what responsible AI actually requires.
